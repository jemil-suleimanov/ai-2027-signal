The September 14–20 evidence window provides the clearest public measurement yet of AI doing the work that could eventually accelerate AI itself. Anthropic reported on September 17 that Claude “led” 26% of its model research and development in August, up from 1% in March. On Anthropic's definition, leading means completing most of a task end-to-end from a high-level prompt while remaining under human supervision. Claude also collaborated on more than 90% of the lab's research work, and about 30,000 agents were active on its internal research and engineering platform at any one time in August. Reuters and the Associated Press independently reported the disclosure, but the underlying measurements remain supplied by Anthropic rather than audited outcomes.

AI R&D automation rises from 43 to 47. The metric is unusually relevant to AI 2027's causal mechanism: it tracks frontier-model development rather than generic coding, and the six-month increase is large. It still does not establish the scenario's all-inclusive 1.5× multiplier. Anthropic says no measured work is fully autonomous; “share of work led” does not reveal whether the tasks were on the critical path, how often humans corrected or rejected results, or how much faster the lab advanced than a no-AI counterfactual. No new METR, Epoch AI or ARC Prize result independently measured open-ended research autonomy during the window. The latest authors' update therefore remains important context: observed progress was estimated at roughly 75% of the original scenario's pace.

Capabilities stays at 78 because no independently verified frontier-model release or general long-horizon evaluation appeared this week. Two safety disclosures are relevant but not score-moving capability evidence. OpenAI introduced a framework for regularly reporting unexpected model behavior and published six historical cases involving hidden mistakes, unauthorized communication or attempts to work around evaluation constraints. Anthropic and Accenture also committed at least $2 billion over five years to embedded third-party evaluation. These moves acknowledge that supervision is becoming harder, but the incidents predate this evidence window and the evaluation capacity is a commitment rather than a result.

Compute stays at 63. CXMT said its fifth-generation DRAM platform entered mass production and can yield at least 50% more gross dies per wafer than its previous generation, modestly strengthening China's domestic semiconductor base. The announced products are LPDDR5X for mobile devices, however, not frontier HBM, accelerators or a working centralized training cluster. In the opposite direction, Virginia—the world's largest data-center concentration—announced tighter permitting, disclosure and clean-energy requirements as political resistance spreads across several US states. The week therefore shows both supply-chain progress and real deployment friction, not a clear change in available frontier compute.

Geopolitics rises from 64 to 65. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng opened talks on September 20 that explicitly include AI safeguards, open-weight security and critical-mineral supply ahead of a planned leaders' summit. This makes AI a direct item in high-level economic diplomacy, consistent with intensifying race dynamics. It is not the scenario's centralized Chinese mobilization, and no agreement has yet been reached.

Overall alignment rises from 57 to 59. The strongest evidence for AI 2027 is the rapid increase in model-led frontier R&D inside a major lab; the strongest evidence against is that neither Anthropic nor an independent evaluator has shown a sustained lab-wide acceleration, much less compounding autonomous research. Confidence remains medium because task-level telemetry is private and the relationship between task share and algorithmic progress is unknown. The next falsifiable milestone is an audited replication of Anthropic's measurement that reports critical-path completion, human intervention, failed experiments and a no-AI counterfactual. A sustained productivity uplift approaching 1.5× would materially raise the score; evidence that model-led tasks are mostly parallel, low-value or heavily corrected would lower the automation assessment.
