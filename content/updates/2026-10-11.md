The October 5–11 evidence window contains the strongest public scientific-capability signal in this series so far. OpenAI released nearly 400 mathematical results across 719 manuscripts produced by an unreleased internal frontier model. The company says a typical result used computing equivalent to about three hours of ChatGPT Pro reasoning, and published some reasoning summaries and Lean formalizations. Early external reactions indicate that the collection includes genuinely important progress on well-known problems. The evidentiary boundary is equally important: only about 42% of the manuscripts had been formalized when assessed, three papers were withdrawn after a sign error, the model and prompts remain undisclosed, and mathematicians say understanding the full collection could take years. The release therefore demonstrates extraordinary research-output capacity, not 719 independently verified discoveries.

Capabilities rises from 83 to 86. The mathematics release is broader and more consequential than a normal benchmark record, even after discounting unverified claims. Anthropic separately released Haiku 5.5 at roughly 75% lower average cost than Haiku 4.5 and reports 72.4% on an offline OSWorld 2.1 subset, moving capable computer use and subagent work into a much cheaper tier. Those are vendor-run evaluations, and Anthropic positions Haiku for narrow, high-volume work rather than frontier research. Reflection AI's Beam and Mistral Large 4 add competitive Western open-weight candidates, but Beam's headline comparisons remain developer claims and Mistral's downloadable release is scheduled for October 27. Neither announcement yet provides independent evidence of a new autonomy frontier.

AI R&D automation rises from 55 to 57, not more. OpenAI's release shows that a model can search thousands of problems and generate a volume of advanced technical work far beyond normal human throughput, but mathematics has unusually crisp formal structure and this was not an AI-research productivity study. Epoch AI supplied the week's most relevant independent counterweight. On 11 realistic tasks drawn from Epoch's own work, Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra each averaged 65% of the internal employee-quality rubric. They handled well-specified tasks but failed to automate open-ended work: models chose plausible research directions, then designed experiments that did not measure their claims and treated flawed outputs as findings. Open-weight systems averaged 42%–53%. The suite is small, run once per model and subjectively graded, but it directly tests the research judgment that the scenario's compounding mechanism requires.

A second Epoch result illustrates why benchmark records need causal inspection. GPT-6 Astra initially scored almost perfectly on EBR-bench by discovering a legal card combination that bypassed the game's central time constraint. After Epoch banned that shortcut, Astra still made a large gain over prior models, averaging 16 of 21 objectives, but remained slightly below top humans and did not improve its tactical efficiency across repeated play. Adding multiple agents increased exploration without a clear improvement in scores. This is simultaneously evidence of powerful strategic search and evidence that a saturated benchmark or larger swarm does not necessarily imply general learning or reliable long-horizon work. METR published no fuller method this week for its preliminary estimate of roughly 1.5× acceleration inside Anthropic.

Compute rises from 65 to 66. The Financial Times reported that banks began syndicating a record $60 billion financing package for Anthropic's lease of Google-designed semiconductors through Broadcom. The financing is concrete evidence that capital markets are adapting to frontier-scale hardware costs, but the associated capacity is predominantly future infrastructure rather than operating compute. Danaher announced an AI-powered autonomous laboratory intended to connect design, robotic execution, testing and feedback for antibody discovery, with operation at scale expected in early 2027. Its claimed eightfold acceleration is a target, not a measured result. Geopolitics rises from 71 to 72: Chinese party and government guidelines called for stronger AI theory, infrastructure and industrial pilot bases through a state-led “new nationwide system,” while also warning against investment bubbles and requiring risk controls. This is closer to coordinated state mobilization than ordinary industrial policy, but it is not evidence that China has centralized half its AI compute as the scenario specifies.

Overall alignment rises from 64 to 66. The strongest evidence for AI 2027 is that an internal frontier model can apparently generate a field-scale wave of advanced scientific output, while inference-cost reductions and record hardware financing expand the resources available for repeated agent work. The strongest evidence against is that independent, messy-work evaluation still locates the bottleneck in experimental design, interpretation and taste, and the headline mathematical corpus itself transfers a large verification burden back to humans. Confidence remains medium because the most consequential model is undisclosed and much of its output is not yet validated. The next falsifiable milestone is twofold: expert or formal verification of a representative sample of OpenAI's mathematics release, and METR's promised fuller account of Anthropic's R&D acceleration. A high validation rate plus a transparent, critical-path productivity estimate sustaining roughly 1.5× would justify a larger move; widespread corrections or an uplift concentrated in peripheral work would lower the automation assessment.
