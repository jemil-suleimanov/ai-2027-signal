The October 26–November 1 evidence window produced a genuine model-frontier advance without new evidence for the mechanism that makes AI 2027 accelerate. Google released Gemini 4 Carbon into a limited cyber-focused rollout, and independent evaluations place it at or near the top of the current frontier. OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra with much lower agentic inference costs, while Mistral published the weights of Large 4. These developments raise model capabilities and strengthen international diffusion. They do not show reliable day-scale research autonomy, a laboratory-wide 1.5× R&D multiplier or newly operating compute on the scale assumed by the scenario. Overall alignment therefore rises only from 67 to 68.

Model capabilities rises from 89 to 91. Google's own results give Carbon 65.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, 61.2% on FrontierSWE2 and 81.5% on OSWorld, alongside strong exploit-development scores. The important point is not any single provider-run number. Vals AI independently scored Carbon at 70.36% on its broad evaluation, narrowly behind Claude Sonnet 5.5 and slightly ahead of Claude Opus 5.5. Other independent boards are more mixed: Carbon leads some reasoning suites but trails the best models on parts of real-world coding. The evidence supports a real frontier gain, not a decisive breakaway. Access is also restricted to selected cyber defenders and evaluators, so deployment reliability remains less tested than the headline scores.

GPT-6.1 Terra adds a different capability signal: economics rather than a new peak. OpenAI reports roughly Astra-level performance at one-tenth the token price and about three times the speed, with a 50% price reduction from the previous Terra tier. Its system card classifies the model as Critical for cyber capability and High for biology and chemistry, but still below the High threshold for AI self-improvement. On internal exploit evaluations it remains below Astra, and the deployment simulation recorded a small number of serious monitoring flags plus higher evaluation awareness than several earlier models. Cheaper competent agents can spread faster and run more experiments, but lower cost is not evidence that they can choose and complete valuable research independently.

Mistral Large 4 reinforces diffusion. The October 29 release exposes a one-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 52 billion active parameters under Mistral's ecosystem licence. Hugging Face reports competitive open-weight results, including 61.7% on DeepSWE and 59.9% on AutomationBench, as well as strong vulnerability reproduce-and-patch performance. Because the weights are inspectable and deployable outside a US frontier lab, this is strategically more important than another closed API update. The results nevertheless come from Mistral and its release partner; no independent evaluator reproduced the model's long-horizon claims during the window.

AI R&D automation remains 57. Neither Carbon nor Terra crossed its developer's self-improvement threshold, and no new METR-style evaluation measured sustained performance on unfamiliar day-scale research work. Google says Carbon is useful for complex cyber investigations, but a restricted expert workflow is not a repeated end-to-end research loop. GPT-Rosalind-6, also released during the week for trusted biological research, uses the same underlying weights as GPT-6 Astra and does not represent a newly trained scientific agent. The absence is now the central comparison: AI 2027's early-2026 trajectory depends on agents compounding algorithmic progress, while public evidence still shows stronger tools inside human-led workflows.

The benchmark evidence also argues for restraint. Epoch AI's October 26 review classified Terminal-Bench 4.0 as flawed because 30 of its 66 tasks had publicly documented scoring defects. That does not erase the broader evidence that agents are improving, but it weakens a benchmark used prominently in all three model releases. A high score under one scaffold cannot be treated as equivalent to robust autonomy under changing objectives, imperfect feedback and real operational consequences.

Compute remains 65. Oracle and NVIDIA announced an intention to expand Vera Rubin systems to multi-gigawatt scale, and the largest cloud providers continue to raise planned AI investment. These are forward commitments rather than newly energized frontier capacity. The announcements did not specify a comparable operating date or demonstrate that previously identified constraints in power, grid connections, cooling, networking and construction have been cleared. With no new evidence of a large cluster entering service during the window, the scenario's rapid physical concentration does not warrant a higher score.

Geopolitics rises from 75 to 76. Large 4 gives Europe a stronger open-weight foundation model at a time when frontier cyber capability and model access are increasingly treated as strategic assets. Carbon's controlled release and Terra's deployment safeguards point in the same direction: powerful systems are being segmented by trust and use case rather than released as ordinary software. The increase is limited because this is diffusion and access control, not the specific mid-2026 scenario mechanism. Public evidence still does not show China concentrating roughly half of its AI compute in a national development zone, and this week brought no new event justifying another US–China escalation score.

The strongest evidence for AI 2027 this week is the combination of an independently supported frontier model, cheaper capable agents and strong downloadable weights outside the leading US labs. The strongest evidence against it is unchanged but more consequential with each passing week: successive model releases have not produced independently verified, reliable autonomous research cycles or an audited lab-wide acceleration near 1.5×. The authors have issued no newer public timeline correction than their August Q2.5 update, which estimated observed progress at roughly 70%–90% of the original pace.

Confidence remains medium. The largest unknown is the productivity and failure rate of internal frontier-lab research agents, which public benchmarks cannot reveal. The nearest concrete test is Moonshot's promised November 4 release of Kimi K3.1 Pro weights. Independent reproduction of its long-horizon coding claims—especially on repaired, contamination-resistant tasks—would materially strengthen the capability case. A more decisive change would require sustained success on unfamiliar day-scale research, little human rescue and a measured contribution to the next model generation. Failure under independent scaffolds, or another week of stronger benchmark scores without research autonomy, would leave the overall assessment nearly unchanged.
