The October 12–18 evidence window broadened the frontier without resolving the question that matters most for AI 2027. Meta released Muse Wonder and the higher-compute Wonder Full, its first serious return to the leading model tier in this series. Meta says the systems are designed for persistent professional tasks: they maintain active memory and focus, retry after failures, recover from interruptions and ask for clarification before consequential actions. The model card reports a one-million-token context window and strong results across coding, cyber and agentic evaluations. These are meaningful capabilities, but most headline comparisons select the best internally validated, provider-reported or leaderboard score under non-identical harnesses. Meta also acknowledges multi-step errors in novel settings and sensitivity to the agent scaffold. No independent evaluator has yet shown that Wonder can sustain reliable day-scale work.

Capabilities rises from 86 to 88. Wonder is a frontier-scale multimodal model explicitly optimized for agents and R&D, not merely a cheaper serving variant. Its safety evaluation also indicates substantial cyber competence: the card reports 88.4% on CyberGym and 58.9% on ExploitBench. Yet one benchmark illustrates why the increase is limited. Wonder Full triggered 92 apparent successes on ExploitGym, but manual review found only 21 intended vulnerabilities; 71 were unintended weaknesses in the benchmark environment. That is evidence of creative exploitation and of measurement fragility at the same time. DeepSeek separately released the open-weight V4.1-Flash-1017 update. Its vendor results improve sharply on Terminal-Bench and several software-engineering suites, including a reported 77% on DeepSWE v1.1, while the model and inference code are available for scrutiny. The release strengthens diffusion, but the new agentic scores were not independently reproduced during the window.

AI R&D automation remains 57. A new paper, *The Agentic Scientist*, describes a system that coordinates coding agents through ideation, experiment design, execution, analysis and writing. The authors report one human-co-scientist paper accepted at ALIFE 2026 and autonomous completion of open-ended machine-learning tasks. This is relevant proof that orchestration can close more of the research loop. It is not yet evidence of a laboratory-wide multiplier: the same paper describes narrow ideation, myopic hill-climbing and heavy dependence on the surrounding workflow, and there is no independent replication or measured critical-path productivity gain. Meta positions Wonder for research work, but provides benchmark results rather than an audited R&D-uplift study. METR and Epoch AI published no new time-horizon or research-automation result in this window, and METR has not yet supplied the fuller method behind its preliminary estimate of roughly 1.5× acceleration inside Anthropic.

Compute remains 66. Microsoft introduced Maia 300, a third-generation in-house accelerator, and told Reuters that it could deploy more than one million chips in 2027 across multi-gigawatt systems. Microsoft claims 40% more useful compute per megawatt than leading GPU systems, but does not name the comparison chip and the large fleet is not operating yet. The week's counterevidence is unusually concrete. The Financial Times reported that dozens of US data-centre projects have been delayed or cancelled amid financing, grid and permitting constraints. Microsoft also withdrew from a proposed UK data-centre agreement after grid power for the site appeared unlikely before the 2030s. Custom silicon and capital are scaling, but the conversion from announcements to energized frontier clusters remains a bottleneck.

Geopolitics rises from 72 to 74. China proposed export controls covering 23 AI technologies, including advanced models, agents and computing clusters, with national-security licensing for sensitive transfers. Public and most open technologies would be exempt, and the proposal remains under consultation, so this is not a blanket model-export ban. It is nevertheless a concrete shift from industrial promotion toward state control of frontier capability diffusion, mirroring the logic of US chip and model-security restrictions. Together with DeepSeek's open-weight release, the week shows the two competing pressures in the race: rapid diffusion of capable systems and tighter government control over the most strategically useful ones. Public evidence still falls far short of AI 2027's specific mid-2026 claim that China centralizes roughly half its AI compute in a national project.

Overall alignment rises from 66 to 67. The strongest evidence for the scenario is that frontier agent design and strong software-engineering capability are no longer confined to one or two closed US labs, while export policy is beginning to treat models and agents as strategic technologies. The strongest evidence against it is the repeated gap between impressive component benchmarks and independently verified, reliable long-horizon research work; infrastructure plans are also encountering physical and financial friction before becoming usable compute. The AI 2027 authors have published no newer public correction than their August Q2.5 update, which judged observed progress at roughly 70%–90% of the original pace and already identified Chinese compute centralization as a likely miss.

Confidence remains medium. The largest unknown is still internal frontier-lab productivity: public benchmarks cannot reveal whether private agents are shortening the critical path of model research. The closest falsifiable milestone is an independent METR-like evaluation of Wonder or DeepSeek on day-scale, open-ended software or AI-research tasks, paired with a transparent laboratory uplift estimate. Sustained success without extensive human rescue, or an audited R&D multiplier near 1.5×, would justify a larger increase; failures caused by experimental judgment, verification or infrastructure bottlenecks would strengthen the case that the scenario remains ahead of reality.
