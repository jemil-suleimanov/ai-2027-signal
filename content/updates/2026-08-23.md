The August 17–23 evidence window produced no new event strong enough to change the assessment. A multi-pass review across frontier releases, agentic systems, AI-assisted AI research, independent benchmarks, compute, deployment, safety, geopolitics, and counterevidence found no newly dated, verifiable frontier model or independent long-horizon result with a material bearing on AI 2027. Older announcements discussed during the week were treated as context rather than new evidence. Capabilities therefore stays at 72.

More importantly, no audited evidence appeared for the scenario's decisive early-2026 mechanism: a sustained, all-inclusive 1.5× acceleration of frontier-lab research. METR's time-horizon work and expenditure-based NanoGPT study remain the most relevant independent frameworks, while Epoch AI and ARC Prize published no new result in this window that demonstrates reliable open-ended research autonomy. Public evidence still supports useful bounded engineering and increasingly long software tasks; it does not show agents selecting research directions, validating experiments, and compounding improvements with low enough failure rates to replace an autonomous research team. Automation stays at 42.

Compute holds at 60. The financing commitments recorded last week remain large, but this window added no verified delivered frontier cluster, major training run, or operating-capacity milestone. Geopolitics holds at 59: advanced Chinese open weights continue to compress practical access to capability, yet no new policy action or public analogue of the scenario's centralized Chinese development zone appeared. Quiet public evidence cannot rule out private progress inside frontier labs, which remains the largest uncertainty and keeps confidence at medium.

Overall alignment stays at 53. The absence of a weekly breakthrough is not itself strong disconfirmation, but repeated weeks without independently measured R&D acceleration gradually raise the burden of proof for the scenario's pace. The authors' latest formal quarterly update remains the Q1 2026 report, with a median fully automated coder date in mid-2028; no newer public correction was found. The closest falsifiable checkpoint remains Z.ai's promised GLM-5.3 release around the end of August. A released model plus independent cyber and multi-day agentic evaluations would move the China-gap and capability tracks; another delay, restricted demo, or unreproduced vendor score would not.
