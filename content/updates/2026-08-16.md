DeepSeek's August 13 release of V4 Pro is the clearest new capability signal in the August 10–16 evidence window. The official model card describes a 1.6-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 49 billion active parameters, a one-million-token context window, and downloadable weights. DeepSeek says the release substantially improves agentic performance. Artificial Analysis independently places it near the leading open-weight tier, but its aggregate score is only a modest improvement over DeepSeek's recent preview models. The result strengthens the evidence for continued Chinese capability diffusion; it does not establish a discontinuous frontier jump or reliable long-horizon autonomy. Capabilities therefore rises from 71 to 72.

The strongest scale-up signal came from NVIDIA's August 10 financing partnerships with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR. The companies aim to mobilize more than $500 billion of third-party capital for AI infrastructure over time. That is meaningful evidence that the financial system is adapting to compute requirements on the scale anticipated by the scenario. It is not $500 billion of deployed capacity: the announcement gives no project-level delivery schedule, and Reuters separately documented growing lender scrutiny and local opposition affecting proposed US data centers. Compute rises from 58 to 60 for financing capacity, with the gap between commitments and operating clusters limiting the move.

The decisive AI 2027 mechanism remains absent from public evidence. No new METR time-horizon result, independently reproduced long-horizon research evaluation, or audited frontier-lab productivity study appeared during the window. METR's earlier NanoGPT expenditure study remains the best controlled evidence: spending more on agents did not reliably convert into equivalent human research value. A retrospective Anthropic risk report published this week covers evidence only through July 15 and is therefore treated as context rather than a new event; it likewise did not find that its automated AI R&D threshold had been crossed. Automation stays at 42.

Geopolitics rises from 58 to 59. A strong downloadable Chinese model narrows the practical diffusion gap even without proving parity at the closed frontier. Z.ai also announced—but did not release—GLM-5.3 and reported mixed internal cyber results. Those claims are not independently testable yet. There is still no public analogue of the scenario's centralized Chinese compute program, so the week supports a competitive race more than its specific state-consolidation mechanism.

Overall alignment rises from 52 to 53. The week adds credible evidence for two background conditions in AI 2027—rapid open-weight diffusion and an extraordinary build-out financing apparatus—but not for the feedback loop that drives its sharp acceleration. The authors' latest formal quarterly update remains the Q1 2026 report, with a median fully automated coder date in mid-2028; no newer public correction was found. The closest falsifiable checkpoint is Z.ai's promised GLM-5.3 release within roughly two weeks. Public weights or trusted-access evaluations that reproduce its cyber claims and show reliable multi-day agentic work would raise both the China-gap and autonomy assessments; a delayed release or weak independent results would leave the signal essentially unchanged.
