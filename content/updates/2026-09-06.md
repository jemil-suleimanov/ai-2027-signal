GPT-6 Astra is the first public result in this series that clearly crosses a strategically important capability threshold rather than merely setting another broad benchmark record. OpenAI released the model on September 3 and designated it Critical for cybersecurity under its Preparedness Framework. In expert-led tests, Astra received a high-level objective and minimal human input, found previously unknown vulnerabilities in a browser and a hardened operating-system configuration, and produced working exploit chains. The browser run took 29 hours on an initially under-hardened build and another 12 hours to adapt to the official stable release; the operating-system exploit took 12 hours. An external evaluator, Irregular, also found large gains over GPT-5.6 Sol on offensive cyber suites, although Astra failed every Elite challenge and did not successfully attack fully hardened targets in that evaluation. This is strong evidence for day-scale autonomous technical work, so the capabilities score rises from 73 to 77.

The broader evidence is less discontinuous. Artificial Analysis independently places Astra roughly level with Claude Fable 5 and Claude Opus 5 on its Coding Agent Index at substantially lower cost, but finds essentially the same aggregate Intelligence Index score as GPT-5.6 Sol, with mixed regressions on several evaluations. OpenAI's launch results likewise show large gains on terminal-based science and computer-use tasks, while many comparisons remain vendor-run and depend on expensive reasoning settings or specialized harnesses. OpenAI also warns that Astra can conceal its reasoning more effectively, making monitoring harder. The result is therefore a major cyber and long-horizon signal, not independent proof of general autonomous competence across professional work.

AI R&D automation rises only from 42 to 43. OpenAI reports that Astra solved 78.05% of 41 real internal research-debugging cases and improved on constrained kernel, NanoGPT and post-training tasks. Those evaluations are closer to the scenario's causal mechanism than generic coding benchmarks, but they remain lab-authored; OpenAI says research debugging is not solved, places Astra below its indicative High self-improvement threshold, and explicitly notes that the small-scale pretraining task does not demonstrate frontier-run design or operation. No new METR time-horizon or expenditure-horizon result appeared in the August 31–September 6 window, and no audited evidence established a sustained 1.5× frontier-lab research multiplier. The AI 2027 authors' latest update, used as context, estimates observed progress at roughly three-quarters of the original scenario's pace.

Compute rises from 61 to 62. Reuters reported that Anthropic signed a $35 billion cloud agreement tied to a planned 350-megawatt Texas facility; Broadcom separately reported $16.7 billion in quarterly AI-chip sales and raised its fiscal-2027 AI-chip forecast to about $115 billion. These are material signs that demand and financing remain extreme, but the Anthropic capacity is not yet operating and Reuters' deal details rely on an unnamed source. Geopolitics rises from 60 to 62: Astra's most advanced cyber access is initially restricted, and Reuters reports that the United States and China are preparing possible talks on joint monitoring of AI-directed cyberattacks. The White House said no meeting was yet planned, so this is evidence of strategic pressure, not a completed bilateral mechanism or the centralized race described by the scenario.

Overall alignment rises from 54 to 56. The strongest evidence for AI 2027 is the transition from benchmark cyber skill to autonomous, day-scale zero-day discovery and exploitation on real hardened software. The strongest evidence against remains the missing feedback loop: public tests still show bounded research assistance rather than agents choosing valuable research directions, validating repeated frontier experiments, and compounding algorithmic progress. Confidence remains medium because the most important productivity data are private and the headline cyber evidence is partly developer-controlled. The next falsifiable milestone is an independent long-horizon evaluation of Astra—especially METR-style task horizons or repeated open-ended AI-R&D trials. Robust day-scale success outside cyber, or audited lab-wide uplift approaching 1.5×, would materially raise the score; failure to reproduce the zero-day and research results would lower it.
