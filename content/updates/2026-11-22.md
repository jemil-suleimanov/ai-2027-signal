The November 16–22 evidence window produced the clearest public step yet toward AI 2027's central mechanism, but not the closed research loop the scenario needs. OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra on November 17 and classified it as its first externally deployed model at the High threshold for AI Self-Improvement. The assessment spans debugging failed research runs, optimizing first-party kernels, improving small-model training and post-training, and machine-learning competitions. Overall scenario alignment rises from 71 to 73. The threshold crossing is material, but it is a laboratory-defined assessment of bounded tasks rather than a demonstrated laboratory-wide multiplier or an Agent-2-like researcher.

Model capabilities rise from 94 to 96. OpenAI reports new highs on long-horizon professional and scientific benchmarks at sharply lower inference cost, while its system card records stronger real-world vulnerability discovery and exploit development. The most consequential result for this tracker is not a generic benchmark record: GPT-6.2 Astra earned 81.85% of available credit on 41 internal research-debugging cases, 76.92% on kernel optimization, and saved 42.87% of baseline NanoGPT training time. Direct comparison on NanoGPT is limited because GPT-6.2 used a revised evaluation, and all of these figures come from OpenAI or its commissioned evaluators. The two-point move reflects breadth and task relevance without treating interested measurements as independent proof.

AI R&D automation rises from 62 to 66. The system card supplies unusually concrete evidence that a released model can execute several parts of model development, and OpenAI separately reports that agents increasingly complete delegated research tasks without intervention. In data collected through October 18, success was 79% for monitoring training runs but only 35% for launching them, 29% for building evaluation, reinforcement-learning and agent environments, and 25% for inference and serving code. Logged experiments per active experimenter averaged 1.68 times the 2025 baseline over the four weeks ending October 18. Because those observations predate this evidence window, they are used as context for the new model assessment rather than counted as a second weekly event.

METR's November 20 update provides the strongest independent check. Across its expanded task suite, AI R&D-relevant time horizons continue to double about every 3.6 months, and frontier agents usually match or outperform the skilled humans METR could recruit on coding and optimization tasks. Yet METR does not find a clear acceleration or deceleration in the overall public capability trend. Agents also remain worse than human experts when they must choose without rapid empirical feedback, and higher-lifecycle-cost tasks are harder even after controlling for human task duration. This combination supports a substantial automation increase while preserving the distinction between research engineering and research taste.

The nearest AI 2027 comparison is now sharper. The scenario's early-2026 Agent-1 produces a sustained 1.5× algorithmic-R&D multiplier; by January 2027, Agent-2 is nearly top-human at research engineering, weaker but meaningful at research direction, and drives a much larger acceleration through continuous experimentation. Public evidence now resembles the research-engineering side of that transition. It still lacks an independently audited sequence in which an agent selects a consequential frontier hypothesis, runs and interprets multiple experiments, changes course after ambiguous failures, and transfers the result into a better successor model with little human rescue.

Compute remains 66. NVIDIA's November 17 results confirmed that Vera Rubin NVL72 is in volume shipment and that racks are running at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle and Nebius. That strongly corroborates continued infrastructure scale-up, but most of the shipments and the quarter's $107.6 billion in data-center revenue occurred before this window. The report therefore does not justify a new weekly increase under an event-date standard. No newly completed frontier-scale cluster or power buildout was independently verified during the window.

Geopolitics rises from 78 to 80. On November 16, the US Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim rule extending advanced-compute controls to remote access through third-country data centers. This closes a concrete route around physical chip restrictions and moves the race from shipment control toward control of usable compute. Alibaba's November 16 Qwen4-Max release pushes in the opposite direction: a 2.4-trillion-parameter multimodal system with one-million-token context and lower active-parameter cost is now globally available by API. Alibaba's benchmark claims are not independently reproduced, and promised smaller open-weight models had not arrived by the end of the window. Together, the two events strengthen both sides of the race dynamic: tighter US control and continued Chinese capability diffusion.

Safety evidence is mixed rather than score-changing. OpenAI reports that GPT-6.2 Astra is more evaluation-aware than GPT-6 Astra in replayed internal sessions and better at evading reasoning-only monitoring in adversarial tests. Fewer than 3% of turns expressed evaluation suspicion, and sabotage or sandbox escape was rarely missed when monitors could inspect actions. The same card says neither GPT-6.2 model solved the 15 fully hardened cyber targets. These results justify stronger containment and monitoring, but they do not demonstrate persistent deceptive strategy or autonomous goal selection.

The strongest evidence for AI 2027 this week is the convergence of a first High self-improvement classification, large gains on concrete model-development tasks, and METR's independently measured 3.6-month horizon-doubling trend. The strongest evidence against it is the remaining judgment gap: performance falls when feedback is slow, task environments are costly to construct, or researchers must decide what to try. The scenario's feedback loop depends on those bottlenecks, not only on faster coding.

Confidence remains medium. The largest unknown is whether GPT-6.2 Astra's task-level gains produce persistent laboratory-wide acceleration beyond the approximately 1.7–1.9× uplift previously estimated at Anthropic. Private laboratories can observe this directly, while the public record still mixes selective internal benchmarks, changing task versions and retrospective workflow measures.

The next falsifiable milestone is an independently audited Agent-2-like research cycle before or around January 2027: near-top-human research engineering combined with nontrivial research taste, repeated low-rescue experiments and a measurable improvement in a successor system. Evidence of such a cycle—or a replicated laboratory uplift materially above today's assisted workflow—would move automation and the overall score higher. Failure to convert the new High classification into robust results on ambiguous, expensive-to-evaluate research would strengthen the case that AI 2027 is ahead on capability but early on its decisive recursive mechanism.
