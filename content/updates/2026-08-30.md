Z.ai met the previous update's nearest checkpoint during the August 24–30 evidence window by releasing GLM-5.3-Flash and downloadable weights. Z.ai describes a 320-billion-parameter multimodal mixture-of-experts model with 18 billion active parameters, hybrid sparse and linear attention, and a one-million-token context window. Its published agentic and professional-work scores are vendor-run and have not yet been independently reproduced. The more scenario-relevant point is operational: Z.ai says it anonymously served the Ox Alpha preview on a large cluster of Chinese accelerators and improved serving performance threefold on the same hardware. This is evidence of improving Chinese capability and reduced dependence on restricted US chips, not proof of parity with the leading closed models. Capabilities rises from 72 to 73 and geopolitics from 59 to 60.

Anthropic's August 27 Model Hardware Standard preview is the week's most concrete agentic-R&D signal. The common interface lets agents orchestrate programmable microscopes, liquid handlers, robotic arms and quantum-control hardware. Partner case studies report narrow closed loops: at QuEra, an agent-derived controller recovered a laser lock 99.3% of the time versus 58% for an earlier bespoke script; at Tetsuwan, Claude helped optimize a laboratory compiler across 9,143 dispenses and improved held-out precision estimates. These are specific, measurable examples of agents executing experiments and adapting parameters, but the evidence comes from Anthropic and launch partners, the tasks remain tightly instrumented, and Anthropic says expert oversight is still required for physical reasoning failures.

That distinction keeps AI R&D automation at 42. MHS lowers an integration bottleneck for scientific automation; it does not show a system autonomously choosing important AI-research questions, designing and validating repeated model-training experiments, or compounding improvements across a frontier lab. No new METR time-horizon or expenditure-horizon result appeared in the window, and no audited evidence established AI 2027's all-inclusive 1.5× research multiplier. The latest authors' update, published August 16 and used here as context rather than new weekly evidence, says observed AI progress is about 75% of the scenario's pace and slightly shortens its Automated Coder forecasts while explicitly noting substantial uncertainty in mapping benchmark horizons to labor replacement.

Compute rises from 60 to 61. NVIDIA's August 26 results confirmed $89 billion in quarterly data-center revenue, production shipments of Vera Rubin beginning in August, and an AWS plan to deploy two million additional GPUs from this quarter through fiscal 2029. These figures show that hardware is moving from financing commitments into products and deployments. The same disclosure is also counterevidence to frictionless exponential scaling: NVIDIA expects supply to remain a bottleneck through fiscal 2028, attributes less than 1% of data-center revenue to licensed Hopper 200 shipments into China, and excludes China data-center compute revenue from its forward outlook. Most of the two-million-GPU commitment is future capacity rather than installed compute.

Overall alignment rises from 53 to 54. The week looks modestly more like AI 2027 in three supporting mechanisms—physical-world agency, domestic Chinese inference, and shipped next-generation hardware—but still not in the feedback loop that makes the scenario accelerate sharply. Confidence remains medium because private frontier-lab productivity data and the identity and scale of Z.ai's accelerators are not independently visible. The closest falsifiable checkpoint is independent GLM-5.3 testing: trusted cyber and multi-day agent evaluations that reproduce Z.ai's claims would raise capabilities and the China-gap assessment. More decisive still would be an audited experiment showing that agents sustain human-competitive returns across repeated, open-ended AI-R&D cycles; another vendor benchmark record would not.
