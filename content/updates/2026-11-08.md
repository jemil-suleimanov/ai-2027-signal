The November 2–8 evidence window produced the clearest public support yet for AI 2027's core acceleration mechanism. After an 11-week high-access engagement inside Anthropic, METR estimated that the laboratory's capability metrics have improved at 1.9 times their earlier rate since October 2025 and judged that a majority of the acceleration is likely attributable to extensive AI use in R&D. This is much stronger evidence than a laboratory press claim: METR examined internal model snapshots, compute histories, research metrics and staff interviews. It is not evidence of autonomous takeoff. The estimate applies to well-specified tasks, important causal details were redacted, robustness checks span a wide range, and METR says the most important parts of model training still lack highly significant fully autonomous contributions. Overall scenario alignment nevertheless rises from 68 to 70 because the mechanism the scenario assigns greatest causal weight has moved from plausible to independently supported.

AI R&D automation rises from 57 to 62. METR's main Anthropic capability index shows a 1.9× acceleration, with a 95% interval of 1.5–2.8×, equivalent to roughly two years of the laboratory's earlier progress in one year. METR says it received convincing evidence that faster growth in training compute did not cause the acceleration. Interviews and internal analyses instead point toward AI assistance, while researchers reported early signs of autonomous experimentation in parts of the pipeline. This is close to the scenario's early-2026 claim that an Agent-1-like system gives the leading lab about a 1.5× algorithmic R&D multiplier.

The comparison is not one-to-one. METR's estimate is a ratio of capability-growth rates, not an audited productivity multiplier for the whole laboratory. Alternative methodological choices produced estimates from 1.1× to 2.9×; internal benchmarks may be saturated or optimized against; self-reported uplift is especially weak evidence; and Anthropic redacted some of the information that most influenced METR's risk judgment. Two of eleven interviewed researchers did not support a greater-than-2× AI acceleration in their area. Most importantly, METR describes the current mechanism as assistance to humans, not reliable end-to-end automation of research direction, implementation, experiment interpretation and model training. The five-point increase therefore recognizes a major evidential update without treating recursive improvement as established.

Model capabilities rises from 91 to 92. OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber on November 5 as a restricted cyber-specialized version of Astra. OpenAI reports that it reproduced 1,724 Linux kernel vulnerabilities, built working privilege-escalation exploits for 948 of them and helped identify 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities; Mozilla has fixed many of the latter. The system card retains Astra's Critical cyber and High biological/chemical designations, but remains below High for AI self-improvement. These are consequential real-system results, yet they are provider-run, domain-specialized and available only to verified security organizations.

Thinking Machines Lab supplied a narrower independent capability signal. ARC Prize verified Inkling 2 Small at 96.5% on ARC-AGI-1 and 81.7% on ARC-AGI-2 at its highest reasoning setting, ahead of every open-weight model ARC had evaluated as of November 6. The weights were still pending at the end of the window, and ARC-style abstraction does not measure long-horizon reliability. Meituan also released LongCat-2.5, a 1.6-trillion-parameter multimodal agent model optimized for domestic accelerators, but its striking agent-benchmark results remain vendor claims. Together these releases justify only a one-point capability increase.

Compute remains 65. Anthropic's November 2 filing disclosed $895.4 billion of contracted computing and infrastructure commitments, including $70 billion due in the next 12 months. A November 8 report also described a $3.4 billion agreement for 1.7 gigawatts of gas-fired generators. These disclosures show that frontier laboratories are trying to convert capital into power and chips at extraordinary scale, but the underlying commitments predate this evidence window and the generators are not operating capacity. Counting them as newly installed compute would confuse contracts with delivered clusters. No verified frontier facility of comparable scale entered service this week.

Geopolitics rises from 76 to 77. Moonshot met its November 4 commitment and published the full Kimi K3.1 Pro weights: a 2.8-trillion-parameter, 104-billion-active multimodal agent model with a one-million-token context window. The release makes a near-frontier Chinese system inspectable and self-hostable, although its size and custom licence sharply limit practical diffusion and its long-horizon results have not been independently reproduced. Separately, Reuters reported on November 3 that US officials were withholding approvals for SK Hynix to install American equipment in its Dalian plant, complicating a proposed $45 billion-plus NAND investment in New York. The episode shows export controls feeding directly back into allied supply-chain decisions. It strengthens race dynamics, but it is still far short of the centralized Chinese compute program described in AI 2027.

The strongest evidence for the scenario is METR's conclusion that Anthropic is probably improving around twice as fast as its earlier baseline and that AI use likely explains most of the acceleration. The strongest evidence against it is contained in the same report: the gains remain concentrated in well-specified, human-directed work, while highly significant autonomous contributions to the most important training stages have not arrived. New cyber exploits, reasoning records and downloadable weights make systems more useful and strategically salient; they do not yet close that autonomy gap.

Confidence remains medium. The largest unknown is whether Anthropic's internal acceleration generalizes across laboratories and persists when benchmarks saturate, bottlenecks shift and AI systems must choose research directions under weak feedback. Private evidence is richer than the public record, but redactions prevent independent replication of the causal attribution. The AI 2027 authors have issued no newer public timeline correction than their August Q2.5 update, which placed observed progress at roughly 70%–90% of the original pace.

The next falsifiable milestone is a public, repeatable demonstration that AI systems can make important autonomous contributions across a full frontier-model research cycle rather than merely accelerate researchers on bounded tasks. An audited result showing low-rescue agents selecting experiments, implementing them, interpreting failures and producing a measurable improvement in the next model generation would move automation and the overall score materially higher. Failure of METR's acceleration estimate to replicate at another frontier laboratory, or evidence that it collapses under non-benchmark outcome measures, would push the assessment back down.
