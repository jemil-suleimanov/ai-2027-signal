The September 21–27 evidence window produced the first public external estimate that directly reaches AI 2027's central 2026 milestone. In its predeployment evaluation of Claude Opus 5.5, METR relayed a preliminary assessment of roughly 1.5× overall acceleration in Anthropic's capabilities research due to AI—equivalent to 1.5 years of progress in one year—with about a 30% chance of 2×. This is materially stronger evidence than last week's lab-reported share of model-led tasks because the evaluator had elevated access to Anthropic's internal R&D process.

The result is not an audit. METR says the separate team that produced the estimate could share its conclusion but not the supporting evidence or detailed reasoning, and the estimate did not specify the period to which it applies. Anthropic reviewed the public summary. METR's own ten-business-day capability evaluation found Opus 5.5 only modestly better than Fable 5.1, with persistent weaknesses in foresight, creating feedback loops and researcher judgement; it judged full AI-R&D automation unlikely. AI R&D automation therefore rises from 47 to 53, not to the level that would imply confirmed compounding takeoff. The score now recognizes a serious external signal while discounting its non-reproducibility and unclear scope.

Capabilities rises from 78 to 80. Anthropic released Opus 5.5 on September 22 with reported gains on agentic coding, computer use and scientific terminal tasks, while cutting typical serving cost by 40% relative to Opus 5. The most relevant evidence is not a single benchmark record but improved economics for long-running work: Anthropic reports a 9.5-hour HAProxy rewrite and outside testers describe unattended sessions lasting 18 hours or more. These remain release-time evaluations and customer reports. METR's finding of incremental rather than discontinuous progress prevents a larger move.

Alibaba added a second, more ambiguous automation signal. Its Apsara materials say Qwen3.8-Max completed 33 automated improvement cycles over a month, raising an Artificial Analysis score from 40 to 45, and ran more than 60 hours of chip-design work with over 10,000 EDA tool calls. The company also announced a 5–10-trillion-parameter model roadmap. These claims are unusually close to the scenario's recursive-improvement mechanism, but Alibaba did not publish trajectories, baselines or artifacts sufficient for independent reproduction. They are treated as interested evidence, not confirmation.

Compute rises from 63 to 64. Alibaba unveiled the Zhenwu V900, claimed three times the performance of its predecessor, with mass production planned for the first quarter of 2027; it also targeted more than 20 GW of global data-center capacity by 2032. The full-stack domestic roadmap modestly strengthens China's path around US chip controls. It does not establish working frontier training capacity today, and Alibaba itself says supply-chain shortages constrain deployment.

Geopolitics rises from 65 to 68. US and Chinese officials agreed to formalize an AI-risk dialogue and pursue an incident line, with another meeting planned in Shenzhen within roughly two months. The agenda explicitly includes uncontrollable agents and cyber incidents. Combined with Alibaba's chip-to-model stack, this makes frontier AI both a state-security issue and a target of Chinese technological self-reliance. It still falls well short of AI 2027's mid-2026 claim that China has centralized half of its AI-relevant compute and most new chips into a single national effort.

Overall alignment rises from 59 to 62. The strongest evidence for AI 2027 is METR's preliminary 1.5× estimate, reinforced by two labs reporting increasingly autonomous R&D loops. The strongest evidence against is the same evaluator's conclusion that the newest public model remains incremental, lacks key research judgement and is unlikely to automate R&D fully. Confidence remains medium because the decisive internal evidence is unavailable and frontier-lab task accounting may not track critical-path algorithmic progress. The next falsifiable milestone is METR's promised fuller output on Anthropic's R&D acceleration. A public method, defined measurement period and task-level counterfactual sustaining approximately 1.5× would materially raise alignment; a lower audited estimate or evidence that gains are concentrated in peripheral work would reverse this week's move.
