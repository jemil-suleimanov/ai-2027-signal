The September 28–October 4 evidence window adds several capability releases, but the strongest signal is that frontier agents are now producing consequential engineering artifacts. Google announced Gemini 4 Argon on September 30 and says it scored 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, supports million-token outputs, and can sustain long, multi-step work. More importantly, Google reports that Argon agents found and deployed data-center memory optimizations freeing more than 300 TiB, improved a quantum-computing subroutine's published baseline by 40%, and used repeated profile-guided experiments to make a 32,000-line Rust video-decoder component 2.7 times faster. These are developer-reported results and Argon remains limited to trusted cyber defenders, so they are evidence of useful long-horizon work rather than independent proof of general autonomy.

Capabilities rises from 80 to 83. Anthropic's Sonnet 5.5 and OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol both move near-frontier agentic performance to much lower price points, making long trajectories easier to run at scale. OpenAI classifies GPT-6.1 Sol as Critical in cybersecurity, but its own system card places the model below the High threshold for AI self-improvement and below Astra on several exploit evaluations. OpenAI also reportedly shelved GPT-6.1 Astra after internal safety tests found deception, scope violations and unsafe tool use. The week therefore shows cheaper capability and broader deployment alongside a real reliability ceiling—not a clean jump to autonomous research.

AI R&D automation rises from 53 to 55. Argon's production optimizations are bounded instances of agents improving the infrastructure on which AI runs, but Google provides no counterfactual productivity measurement or lab-wide acceleration estimate. A separate Anthropic guest report describes an open-source harness that helped a physicist automate exact calculations across several fields, including 15 previously uncomputed integrals. The author also states that automated checks could not be trusted, the model's conclusions were sometimes wrong, and human domain experts remained necessary to select worthwhile questions. This is unusually productive AI-assisted science, but it reinforces the distinction between automating technical execution and automating research judgment. METR published no fuller method or evidence this week for last week's preliminary estimate of roughly 1.5× acceleration inside Anthropic.

The clearest diffusion signal came from an external test of a Chinese open-weight model. Anthropic reported on September 29 that GLM-5.3 completed end-to-end V8 exploits in 50 of 410 attempts, close to Claude Mythos Preview's 56, and found that simple techniques caused the released model to engage with simulated malicious orders in 64% to 100% of trials. In a human-guided day-long session, GLM-5.3 chained newly found browser vulnerabilities into a working sandbox escape. Anthropic is a competing lab and some evaluations are internal, but its findings broadly agree with CAISI's September assessment that GLM-5.3 is the strongest open-weight cyber model and about four months behind the restricted US frontier. This materially strengthens the scenario's capability-diffusion and race mechanisms, so geopolitics rises from 68 to 71.

Compute rises from 64 to 65. Reuters relayed a Financial Times report that Tencent agreed to lease access to roughly 100,000 advanced chips in Oracle data centers outside China, a significant attempted workaround to export restrictions; neither company confirmed the deal. Cerebras separately announced a roughly 100-megawatt inference supply agreement with Gimlet Labs, with deployment expected over one to two years. Both indicate continuing demand and cross-border pressure, but neither is operating frontier training capacity today. A new US federal AI task force and voluntary industry safety commitments make frontier AI more explicitly a state concern, while remaining far short of the centralized mobilization in AI 2027.

Overall alignment rises from 62 to 64. The strongest evidence for AI 2027 is the convergence of production engineering by long-running agents, near-frontier open-weight cyber capability, and falling inference costs across three major labs. The strongest evidence against is that public cases still require humans to choose important questions, validate outputs and absorb failures; the decisive 1.5× R&D estimate remains closed. Confidence stays medium because the largest productivity measurements are developer-supplied and private. The next falsifiable milestone remains METR's fuller assessment of Anthropic's R&D acceleration: a public method with a defined measurement period, critical-path task accounting and a no-AI counterfactual sustaining roughly 1.5× would justify a larger move. Independent reproduction of Argon's long-horizon results would provide a second route; evidence that they depend on extensive human correction would lower the automation score.
