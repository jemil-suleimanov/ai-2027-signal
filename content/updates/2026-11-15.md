The November 9–15 evidence window produced a large capability signal but a smaller update to AI 2027's causal story. Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 on November 9, and two independent evaluators placed it at the frontier: Epoch AI gave it a record Epoch Capabilities Index score of 173, while ARC Prize verified 93.8% on ARC-AGI-2 and 81.2% on ARC-AGI-3 with its standard harness. Anthropic also reports that the model can work on ambiguous technical projects for days, orchestrate subagents and recover from setbacks. Overall scenario alignment rises from 70 to 71. The evidence makes advanced agents more capable and persistent, but it does not add a second independent measurement of the compounding AI-R&D mechanism that drove last week's larger increase.

Model capabilities rises from 92 to 94. Fable 5.2's ARC-AGI-3 result is especially relevant because the benchmark requires interaction over many steps rather than a single answer. The result is not equivalent to reliable real-world autonomy: the highest-scoring provider harness preserves opaque reasoning state and compacts long conversations, and 28 of 55 games at the maximum standard setting exhausted the full 12-hour limit. Anthropic's project examples—software migrations, physical-device design, black-hole physics and a 30-hour formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem—are provider claims rather than independent reproductions. Even with those caveats, the combination of Epoch's broad index and ARC Prize's task-level testing warrants a two-point increase.

Google supplied a second, differently structured signal. AlphaProof Code used Gemini Carbon agents to generate more than six million lines of Lean and formally verify key security properties of Signal's production cryptographic implementation in four weeks, a task Google says historically takes expert teams years. Human experts chose and reviewed the specifications, so this was not autonomous research direction, but the proof checker makes the final technical artifact unusually verifiable. Gemini Robotics 2.5 also expanded the range of physical tasks demonstrated across more than 200 evaluations; Google reports 96% pick-and-pack success in a Mercedes-Benz proof of concept. These results broaden the domains in which long agentic workflows can produce checked outputs, although both remain laboratory-led demonstrations.

AI R&D automation remains 62. Anthropic explicitly says it altered Fable 5.2's training to limit progress in accelerator-kernel optimization and large-language-model architecture research, leaving those skills near Opus 5.5 while preserving general coding, hardware and traditional machine-learning abilities. This is significant evidence that governance can shape the takeoff-relevant capability profile. It also means Fable's general leap should not be treated as a direct increase in recursive self-improvement capacity. No new independent evaluator showed an agent choosing a frontier research direction, implementing and interpreting a sequence of experiments, and carrying the result into the next model generation with little human rescue.

The AI Futures Project published a major correction on November 10 in response to METR's November 5 Anthropic assessment. The authors now estimate roughly 1.7× AI-attributable acceleration in the current pace of progress, a level their August models assigned less than 4% probability for November 2026. Their updated all-things-considered median forecasts for top-expert-dominating AI moved to between September 2027 and September 2028, and several methods place a superhuman AI researcher in 2027. This materially strengthens the interpretation that observed reality is running ahead of the original model's expected intermediate pace. It is not a new measurement: the update reinterprets the same partly redacted METR evidence already scored last week, and the authors acknowledge that a non-AI change in baseline productivity could produce similar aggregate acceleration without implying fast takeoff. Automation therefore stays flat.

Compute rises from 65 to 66. Nebius reported on November 12 that its first Vera Rubin systems are live and being integrated into its cloud, that four new sites began generating revenue in the third quarter, and that it delivered additional New Jersey capacity through November. This is delivered hardware rather than a memorandum or future power reservation. The increase remains only one point because Nebius did not disclose the size of the live Rubin deployment, customer-scale use is still ahead, and some colocation capacity shifted into the first half of 2027. The evidence supports continued scale-up while preserving the distinction between early systems and completed gigawatt frontier clusters.

Geopolitics rises from 77 to 78. Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Small's Apache-2.0 weights on November 12 after external safety testing, and Meituan released the LongCat-2.5 weights under MIT terms on November 9. Neither event creates a new frontier model—the hosted systems were introduced earlier—but both turn capable multimodal agents into inspectable, self-hostable artifacts and accelerate international diffusion. At the same time, Reuters reported that three bills to tighten advanced-chip exports, tracking and allied equipment controls had advanced in the US House and entered Senate defense-bill negotiations. The bills were not law by the end of the window, and a separate report said the administration had delayed a new diversion crackdown. The one-point increase reflects intensifying institutional pressure, not a completed US–China compute separation or the centralized Chinese program described in the scenario.

Google's Gemini 4 Hydrogen reinforces the economic diffusion signal but does not change the score. It targets cheaper, faster repeated agent work rather than a new reasoning frontier. Google's official model card shows strong automation and computer-use results but mixed performance against larger models on scientific terminal tasks. Systems in this class make it easier to deploy agents at scale; they do not demonstrate that agents remain reliable when feedback is weak and research goals are ambiguous.

The strongest evidence for AI 2027 this week is the conjunction of a new independently measured capability frontier, day-scale technical work and the scenario authors' sharp update toward a 2027 superhuman-researcher window. The strongest evidence against it is more specific and still decisive for the score: the most advanced public model was deliberately prevented from advancing the AI-R&D skills most directly tied to recursive improvement, while the best verified long workflows still depend on human specification, scaffolding or formal checkers.

Confidence remains medium. The largest unknown is whether the 1.7–1.9× acceleration measured at Anthropic persists across laboratories and translates from human-directed assistance into low-rescue research autonomy. Private laboratories have better evidence than the public record, but redactions, selective demonstrations and changing benchmark scaffolds prevent a clean comparison with the scenario's laboratory-wide multiplier.

The next falsifiable milestone is the scenario's January 2027 transition toward an Agent-2-like system: a model that is near top-human level at research engineering, has nontrivial research taste and drives a multiplier materially above today's assisted workflow. A public or independently audited full-cycle experiment—agent-selected hypotheses, implemented trials, correct interpretation of failures and a measurable improvement in a successor model—would move automation and the overall score materially higher. If the new capability records fail to produce such low-rescue results, or if Anthropic's acceleration does not replicate outside one laboratory, the gap between broad intelligence and recursive R&D will become stronger counterevidence.
